# NEURAL080: video dynamics versus eye movements in EEG

Investigate whether a prespecified representation of time-varying visual appearance improves prediction of EEG after accounting for chosen static, motion, acoustic, trial-timing and ocular predictors. This is a new **descriptive encoding comparison**, not the published Figure5 analysis, a purely visual mechanism test or proof of uniquely dynamic neural information.

## Frozen predictor definitions

No learned visual/acoustic network or pretrained weights are part of this contrast. The question is conditional on the following explicitly limited low-level representations; a positive gain does not show semantics, full acoustic control or removal of every ocular/motion explanation.

The visual representation uses all pixels of each decoded RGB frame, partitioned into8×8 spatial cells without aspect-ratio rescaling. Each cell is its RGB mean divided by255. Use actual frame start/end times and duration weighting to obtain30 consecutive100ms appearance bins over[0,3). Do not guess nominal frame rate, repeat a last frame or substitute a static image. Missing coverage fails preparation. Actual video-ID/file mappings and decoded presentation-clock provenance are required; those inputs are currently absent.

- **Static:**per-cell RGB temporal mean and population SD across all30 appearance bins,384 values.
- **Motion:**absolute consecutive-bin changes in the mean of the three RGB cell values,30×64 values. The first bin is zero because it has no preceding within-clip bin. This is an unsigned coarse appearance-change proxy, not optical flow or a complete motion model.
- **Dynamic:**the30×192 chronological appearance values after subtracting each cell/color's temporal mean. This isolates ordered deviations relative to this static mean, not high-level event understanding.
- **Acoustic:**for each100ms interval, RMS, zero-crossing fraction and log1p power in eight equal-width frequency bands from0 to the actual Nyquist frequency. Use native decoded sample rate, channel-averaged powers and the explicit first-sample presentation time; do not resample or invent silence for missing audio. A silent authenticated track is valid. These features do not exhaust speech or sound semantics.
- **Trial timing:**per-video proportions of joined repeats in each of eight sessions and16 runs, plus mean and population SD of within-run trial position `(trial−1)/65`,26 values. This is source repeat-context information, not EEG-derived quality. The target coordinate's time is fixed by the supplied output axis.
- **Ocular:**for each native20ms interval, three observed means of source x-gaze, y-gaze and pupil values; three mean absolute adjacent-sample derivatives; observed-sample fraction; and valid adjacent-pair fraction. Derivatives use only adjacent observed samples within the same interval and the500Hz source clock. No interpolation, wraparound or extra eye-trial rejection. A completely missing interval contributes zeros plus explicit zero availability. Average these trial-level features over the **same joined repeats** used for the EEG target, retaining1,200 values per video. Released gaze/pupil units and baseline processing are retained; no conversion to a new physical scale is made.

Feature scaling uses only the900 fit identities for that person. Exact constant fit columns remain zero in every partition, including query-only deviations. Anchored, scaled calculations preserve near-constant variation without declaring roundoff to be information. No query-fitted PCA, scaling, hyperparameter choice, feature selection or calibration is permitted.

## Metrics and interpretation

The primary is **full-arm MSE**, lower is better:equal weight across the102 videos and all127×150 fixed coordinates, then equal weight across all six participants. It ranks predictive error, not dynamic contribution. Every arm's absolute MSE is retained for every participant.

Report signed participant-paired contrasts:nuisance-eye minus full MSE (dynamic gain given ocular predictors); nuisance minus nuisance-dynamic MSE (dynamic gain without ocular predictors); nuisance-dynamic minus full MSE (ocular gain given dynamic predictors); attenuation of the dynamic gain after adding ocular predictors; and shifted-full minus full MSE. No sign or significance threshold is an admission condition. Weakening controls can inflate a contrast, but cannot improve the ranked full-arm error.

Use10,000 whole-participant bootstrap draws, seed80057, sharing the same six-person draw schedule across contrasts. Report pointwise2.5/97.5 percentiles descriptively, conditional on the fixed102 source videos and released trial availability. These are not simultaneous confidence guarantees, population-video uncertainty or multiple-comparison-controlled significance tests. Six participants give limited tail resolution; repeated videos, trials, channels and time bins do not increase the participant count.

Participant03 remains in the six-person primary. The source documents eye-calibration concerns associated with glasses. Always report the fixed leave-participant03-out sensitivity separately; never select which result to emphasize based on its direction. This sensitivity does not establish that the other five ocular measurements are error-free.
