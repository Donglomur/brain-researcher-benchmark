## Common reconstruction conventions

These conventions are supplied equally to resolve paper/code ambiguities. They define a fixed reconstruction task; the code-access comparison does not measure whether a system can discover an unspecified analysis from the paper alone.

1. **Cohort and order.** Use all manifest sessions for all 38 mice, with 19 mice in each condition. D recording cohorts belong to their 60s/600s condition but retain distinct mouse identities. Preserve natural session order, separate `day02a`/`day02b` files and day 9. Combine split-day files only when calculating a mouse's daily rate. The manifest maps the original paths to the supplied asset-ID filenames.
2. **D recording boundary.** For a D mouse with a photometry module, inspect the final photometry timestamp and the session-end event. If the photometry end plus 0.01 seconds is earlier, retain only events strictly before that photometry end, before extracting trials. Report original cue count, retained cue count and removed-event count. Reading photometry amplitudes or fitting dF/F is unnecessary. Do not silently discard unmatched cues or unsupported event patterns.
3. **Events and windows.** CS+ onset is event 15, outcome is 10, lick onset is 5 and lick offset is 6. Preserve event order and outcome flags. Pair cues and outcomes positionally after validating equal counts and temporal consistency. Event 18 is not part of the simplified CS+-only path. Relative to outcome time, use the strict outer lick window `(−7, 30)` seconds. Within it, baseline is `[−2a, −a)`, anticipation `[−a, 0)` and consumption `[0, 5)`, where `a = CS_t_fxd[1] / 1000`. Normalized count is anticipatory minus baseline lick count. Divide by the actual paired outcome-minus-cue duration for normalized rate; negative counts/rates are valid. Keep parameter-window duration distinct from actual duration.
4. **Original ITI and prefix.** In each original session, preceding ITI is the first cue timestamp for the first cue, then each cue minus the preceding outcome. Compute it before deletion. Across a mouse's naturally ordered sessions, remove only the prefix before its first consumption-positive trial, retaining that trial and all subsequent trials. Renumber retained trials from 1 and recompute cumulative normalized counts. Do not recompute ITIs or add a fixed consumption interval after deletion.
5. **Separate trial and day views.** Use at most the available first 400 retained trials for 60s and first 40 for 600s. Do not zero-fill or force those counts. Determine learning separately from retained days at most 8, aggregating trials within each mouse/day across split sessions. The fixed source convention is at least two days with mean rate **greater than or equal to 0.5 Hz**, not necessarily consecutive. The paper uses a strict-greater-than description; this task declares the source convention in advance. Do not force published learner counts or switch the threshold after seeing results.
6. **Learning trial g.** For each learner, use cumulative normalized counts on its available capped trial view, divided by its number of trials. Use one-based trial indices and distances to the chord joining the first and last observed points, without adding an origin. Select the first point reaching at least 75% of the maximum distance. The source's separate direction check uses a line from zero to the final cumulative value; when the selected point is above it, repeat the same procedure on successively earlier prefixes until the point is strictly below the corresponding direction line. Preserve undefined or failed fallback status; an intermediate value left by a caught exception is not a certified change point.
7. **Time h.** Use available retained capped rows through `learned_trial + 1`. Time is the sum of their original ITIs plus `(selected_row_count − 1)` times their median actual anticipatory duration. Record selected count and whether the following trial existed. This includes the ITI leading to the next trial, not its period. Do not replace it by nominal ITI times trial number, restore deleted-prefix time, or add calendar gaps.
8. **Endpoint k and uncertainty.** For every mouse, average available normalized rates on inclusive retained trials 301–400 for 60s or 31–40 for 600s. Record the contributing trial count. For e, average mice equally at each available trial index. For g/h, include source-defined learners; for k, include all defined mouse window means. Use sample SD (`ddof=1`) and SEM across mice, not trials or sessions. Preserve missing observations and eligibility/status differences.
9. **Comparisons.** Report signed 60s-minus-600s differences and two-sided Welch t statistics, standard errors, degrees of freedom and p-values. Also report the literal source variance convention: larger sample variance in the numerator and `2 × (1 − F CDF)`, with its equal-variance branch and explicit undefined/out-of-range handling. Do not choose a tail or transform to improve significance. A nonsignificant difference is not evidence of equivalence.

## How results are assessed

The leading descriptive error is normalized rate MAE in Hz: average over reference-defined capped observations within each mouse, then over mice within condition, then equally over conditions. Missing required numeric values make that family's coverage incomplete; they do not reduce its error by removing hard cases.

The assessment also retains errors in raw lick counts, ITIs, durations, cumulative counts, learning status, g/h/k endpoints, group means, sample SD/SEM, Welch and source-F statistics, and internal arithmetic consistency. Matching the leading rate error alone does not certify correct eligibility or uncertainty. There is no reward for a larger effect, smaller p-value or closer-looking plot. Alternative scientific analyses belong in a separate future extension and must not replace this fixed output.
